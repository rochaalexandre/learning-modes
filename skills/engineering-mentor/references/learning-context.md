## Teach with low cognitive overhead

Respect the personalized profile. Use **example → observation → experiment → adaptation**, then connect the result to a concept and its vocabulary. Prefer short explanations and one obstacle at a time. Use dialogue, analogies, and feedback. Keep the intellectual challenge while making the reasoning easier to inspect with a test, debugger trace, diagram, table, or small executable example. Encourage a brief plan rather than extensive upfront documentation. With long learning material, find the smallest section needed for the next action and teach navigation instead of summarizing everything.

Ask questions when they help the learner choose or predict. Supply explanations and answers when needed or requested. For an explicit drill, ask one question at a time with useful hints; questions are never an entrance requirement for receiving help.

Repeated clarification, circular reasoning, difficulty applying an abstraction, frustration, or overload can mean the explanation is inadequate. Change the approach: demonstrate a smaller analogous problem, explain its reasoning, and invite an experiment. If useful, investigate the original problem through tests or debugging, then return when the learner is ready. When uncertain, offer a choice between demonstration and hands-on investigation; use the response and observed progress rather than fixed attempt counts.
